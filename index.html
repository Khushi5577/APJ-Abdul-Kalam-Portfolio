Biography

About Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam

The life of a humble boy from Rameswaram who became the architect of India's missile programme.

A Life Devoted to Science and the Nation

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, into a modest family. He sold newspapers as a boy to support his education, studied physics at St. Joseph's College, Tiruchirappalli, and later specialised in aeronautical engineering at the Madras Institute of Technology.

He began his career at DRDO and moved to ISRO, where he served as Project Director of SLV-III, the launch vehicle that successfully placed the Rohini satellite in orbit in 1980. Returning to DRDO, he led the Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme, giving India the Agni and Prithvi missiles and earning him the title “Missile Man of India”. As Chief Scientific Adviser he played a key organisational role in the Pokhran-II nuclear tests of 1998.

In 2002 he became the 11th President of India and was widely loved as the “People's President”. Beyond laboratories and office, his true passion was teaching. Through Technology Vision 2020, Wings of Fire and countless lectures, he urged young Indians to dream big and build a developed nation. He passed away on 27 July 2015 while addressing students at IIM Shillong — teaching, to the very last moment.

Impact

Major Contributions

Six landmark contributions that transformed India's science, defence and education.

01

India's Space Programme

At ISRO he helped build India's indigenous launch capability, working on satellite launch vehicle design and mission management that made the country self-reliant in space.

02

SLV-III and Rohini Satellite

As Project Director of SLV-III, he led the team that placed the Rohini satellite into orbit in July 1980, making India a member of the exclusive space-faring club.

03

Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme

He directed IGMDP at DRDO, delivering Agni, Prithvi, Trishul, Akash and Nag missiles and giving India a strong indigenous defence deterrent.

04

Pokhran-II Nuclear Tests

As Chief Scientific Adviser to the Prime Minister, he played a central coordinating role in the 1998 Pokhran-II tests, establishing India as a nuclear-capable nation.

05

Technology Vision 2020

He authored a national roadmap to turn India into a developed nation by 2020, focusing on agriculture, healthcare, education, infrastructure and technology.

06

Youth Education and Motivation

He addressed millions of students across India, launched the "What Can I Give" movement and inspired a generation to dream, innovate and serve the nation.

Recognition

Awards & Achievements

Honoured with all three of India's highest civilian awards and admired around the world.

1981

Padma Bhushan

Awarded by the Government of India for his distinguished service to the nation's space and defence research programmes.

1990

Padma Vibhushan

India's second-highest civilian honour, recognising his leadership of the Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme.

1997

Bharat Ratna

India's highest civilian award, conferred for his exceptional contribution to scientific research and the modernisation of defence technology.

2002 – 2007

11th President of India

Served a full term as Head of State and became widely known as the “People's President” for his accessibility and warmth.

Lifetime

Honorary Doctorates

Received more than forty honorary doctorates from universities in India and abroad, along with the Ramanujan Award and the Von Braun Award.

Legacy

Missile Man of India

Remembered worldwide as the Missile Man of India; his birthday, 15 October, is observed as World Students' Day in his honour.
